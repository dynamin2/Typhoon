# Citation discipline

## Never write a citation you have not retrieved

Every citation must correspond to a record actually returned by a search this
session. No reconstructing a reference from memory, no filling in a plausible
author/year for a finding you are confident about. If a claim needs a citation
and no retrieved record supports it, write the claim and mark it
`[CITATION NEEDED]` rather than inventing one.

## Verify before use

For each citation, confirm from the retrieved record: first author, year,
journal, and that the abstract actually contains the claim being attributed. A
DOI that resolves is not sufficient — a real paper cited for something it does
not say is the more common and more damaging error.

## Distinguish showed from claimed

When summarizing a paper, separate what the data support from what the authors
concluded. For imaging and synaptic physiology specifically, note the
preparation (culture, slice, in vivo), the species, and the indicator or method
used — findings in these fields frequently do not transfer across them, and a
summary that omits the preparation is misleading even when technically correct.

## Flag contested findings

Where a claim is disputed in the literature, say so and cite both sides rather
than presenting the majority view as settled.

## Preprints

Mark preprints as such, with the server and posting date. Do not present a
preprint result at the same evidentiary weight as a peer-reviewed one, and
check whether it has since been published.
